Real recessions usually had three or four signals on, at least partly; false alarms usually had one or two, almost always the yield curve and the leading index. Credit spreads and jobless claims stayed calm in nearly every false alarm, which is why they are the confirming signals. They also tend to move late, often after stocks have started falling. *The 2019 inversion before COVID was coincidental. The historical tally used the Conference Board LEI; this page uses the free OECD leading indicator instead.

Data: FRED T10Y3M, FRED BAMLH0A0HYM2 (ICE BofA US High Yield OAS), FRED ICSA, FRED SAHMREALTIME, and the OECD composite leading indicator. Updated daily by a GitHub Action.

This is an educational indicator dashboard, not investment advice. Recession signals have long and variable lead times, they produce false alarms, and they do not predict shocks like a pandemic.
